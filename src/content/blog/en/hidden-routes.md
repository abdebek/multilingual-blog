Türkiye's famous destinations—Istanbul's Old City, Cappadocia's balloons, Antalya's coast—deserve their reputation. But they have a side effect: the traveller who only sees them leaves with a narrow picture of an enormous and varied country. The routes below are not "secret" in the absolute sense—locals know them well—but they reward the visitor willing to drive an extra hour past the obvious stop.

## Gökçeada's Greek Villages

In the Aegean, off the western coast near Çanakkale, Gökçeada is Türkiye's largest island and one of its calmest. Its old Greek stone villages—Tepeköy, Zeytinliköy, and Dereköy—sit on slopes above the sea, their cobbled lanes and shuttered houses largely untouched by mass tourism. Zeytinli köy in particular is worth a slow afternoon: olive groves, a couple of family-run meyhanes, and a silence that is increasingly rare within an hour's flight of a major city.

Bring a car on the ferry from Kabatepe, stay a night, and eat whatever the village kitchen has caught or picked that day.

## The Artvin Highlands

Far in the northeast, Artvin is the Türkiye most tourists never see. The Karçal Mountains and the Borçka Karagöl nature reserve are draped in every shade of green, with alpine lakes fed by snowmelt and hiking routes that see more shepherds than visitors. The yayla (highland) culture here—seasonal migration to upland pastures—is still lived, not performed for cameras.

It is not an easy destination. Roads are winding, weather shifts fast, and infrastructure is modest. For travellers who measure a trip by what they noticed rather than what they consumed, that is the point.

## Birgi: A Village Frozen in Time

In İzmir's Ödemiş district, the village of Birgi preserves architecture from the Aydınoğulları principality—ornate wooden houses, the Çakırağa Mansion, and the Birgi Ulu Camii with its distinctive tiles. It is a single-street village that can be walked in an hour and thought about for days. Unlike the restored-for-Instagram villages elsewhere, Birgi feels inhabited, its slow decay part of the appeal rather than hidden from it.

Pair it with a drive up to the Bozdağ mountains for the contrast between lowland history and highland air.

## How to Travel These Routes Well

- **Go slow.** These places reward two nights, not a photo stop. The value is in the morning walk before the one café opens.
- **Rent a car.** Public transport exists but is built for locals on local schedules, not for fitting four destinations into a long weekend.
- **Spend locally.** A family pension and a village kitchen do more for these places than any development project. Your dinner is also their economy.

The best travel advice for Türkiye in 2026 is the same as it has always been: go one step past where the crowd stops. The country is bigger than the brochure.
